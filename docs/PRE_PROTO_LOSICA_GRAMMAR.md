# Late Pre-Proto-Losica reference grammar

## Historical position

Late Pre-Proto-Losica represents the first reconstructible language of the
Losican family. Its period begins in an undated past and ends around c. 800.
Proto-Losica develops through the documented ordered sound changes and divides
into Komuheftic and Sisengwigwo around c. 1036. The existing generated roots
belong to the Pre-Proto stage and supply the historical input to later forms.

## Speakers and setting

The reference community occupies an irregular southern maritime coast around
53 degrees south, 56 degrees east. Peninsulas, islands, coves, river mouths,
wetlands, freshwater sources, and stone deposits organize daily movement. The
modeled homeland receives about 1,053 mm of precipitation per 442.61-day orbit,
equivalent to an 869 mm annual rate on Earth. Wetter hot seasons alternate with
drier cool seasons.

The speakers are anatomically ordinary humans with local physiology for 1.18 g
gravity and an oxygen partial pressure of about 12.85 kPa. Adaptations include
efficient oxygen use, stronger load-bearing anatomy, and cardiovascular
regulation. Their senses, cognition, reproduction, and development follow the
ordinary human range.

One mean solar turn lasts 8.556787 Earth hours. A human wake-sleep cycle spans
about three solar turns: approximately 17 hours awake followed by 8.5 hours of
sleep. Principal sleep therefore recurs around every third dark interval.
Vocabulary distinguishes a solar turn, a dark interval, a sleep period, and a
human wake-sleep cycle. `putmar` names the next human wake-sleep cycle.

## Community

Late communities are mobile coastal foragers. Controlled fire, shaped stone
tools, wooden implements, cordage, temporary shelters, rafts, and near-shore
boats form their cumulative technology. Bands follow seasonal circuits among
recurring camps. Fresh water, sheltered coves, stone sources, and crossings
give each camp a stable social identity.

Named clans link multiple bands. Clan membership is matrilineal: children
receive their mother's clan. Partnership rules and childcare organization are
unresolved. Political authority is situational at band scale.

Neighbouring bands meet regularly for companionship, partner exchange,
toolstone exchange, shared activity, and route information. This network forms
one speech continuum with local accents and preferred expressions. The later
Komuheftic-Sisengwigwo divergence grows from these regional patterns.

Fauna and flora remain available for the planetary biosphere design. The
current lexicon uses generic ecological categories such as tree, leaf, wood,
food, water, sea, stone, sand, mud, rain, and fire. External language contact
has status `unresolved`; etymological records may use `contact_possible` for an
irregular root pending comparative evidence.

## Morphological profile

Late Pre-Proto-Losica is predominantly isolating. Roots and grammatical
particles are invariant free forms. The productive status and ordering of
compounding remain unresolved.

The older `config/language_profile.json` drives the legacy typological
generator. It describes a later agglutinative design space. This reference
grammar governs the historical Losican stage represented by the working
language.

## Nominal phrases

Number is expressed when discourse requires it. Independent particles mark a
patient, recipient or beneficiary, and spatial or temporal ground:

| Form | Function |
|---|---|
| `tu` | patient, especially where role recovery would be ambiguous |
| `lap` | recipient or beneficiary |
| `qit` | location or time ground |
| `ruk` | proximal deixis |
| `ut` | distal deixis |

### Reference, possession, and clan evidence

The documented source form `muto` denotes the current utterance source. Because
Pre-Proto Losica has no `/o/`, the general adaptation of documented incoming
forms maps `o` to `u`; the Pre-Proto form is therefore `mutu`. It may occur as
an ordinary referring expression, including in the position occupied by a
person's name.

No other reference word is presently documented. English reference words in
the input language therefore receive no invented Pre-Proto forms.

The internal structure of nominal possession and kin expressions remains
unresolved. Matrilineal clan membership is established. Clan exogamy,
childcare organization, and derived clan-kin vocabulary remain unresolved.
Vocabulary admission is limited to existing documented roots.

## Clause structure

The ordinary clause template is:

```text
(topic) subject patient-tu recipient-lap location-qit
polarity time aspect mood verb question
```

SOV is the default constituent order and postpositions follow their nominal
complements. Discourse-recoverable arguments may be omitted. `nap` closes a
question. `run` marks negative polarity, `pun` locates an event before the
reference point, `kit` presents a bounded or completed event, and `nir`
expresses potential or ability.

Unmarked predicates are tenseless. Time words and discourse locate an event in
the past, present, or future. `tam` marks the prospective aspect:

> an event is approaching, intended, planned, or expected from the current
> reference point.

The lexical source of `tam` is the root "be next; approach; be on the point of
occurring." Its development is:

```text
independent predicate -> serial event predicate -> event modifier -> PROSP
```

With a deliberate first-person subject, `tam` commonly conveys intention. With
another person it conveys a plan or expectation. With weather or an inanimate
subject it conveys a prediction based on present signs. A time word can locate
a future event by itself; `tam` adds the speaker's prospective construal.

## Spatial organization

The maritime setting supports productive spatial expressions for seaward,
landward, uphill, downhill, and movement along either coastal direction. These
expressions combine ordinary place, direction, motion, sea, land, and slope
roots. Repeated routes can conventionalize shorter compounds during the
transition to Proto-Losica.
